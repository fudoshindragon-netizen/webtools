Wire → breaker rule

  1. Find the conductor’s ampacity at the temperature rating of the termination — not the highest column on the chart.
  2. The breaker rating must not exceed that ampacity.
  3. Choose the largest standard rating at or below that number.

Standard ratings are a fixed ladder of preferred sizes, not every whole number — which is why an ampacity of 65 A or 85 A does not correspond to a standard breaker. The ladder itself, and the limited allowances for specific cases, are in the adopted code; they are not reproduced here.

Source The general rule is described in NEC 240.4 (Article 240); the code text itself was not read for this printing, and the 240.6(A) ladder is not printed here.

What the columns mean

60 °CConductors and terminations limited to 60 °C. This is the column the manufacturer chart assigns to NM-B and UF-B.
75 °CTerminations rated 75 °C — most modern equipment — and types THW, THWN, SE, USE, XHHW.
90 °CTypes THHN, THWN-2, XHHW-2, USE-2. Not a licence to size the breaker at 90 °C: terminations and equipment ratings cap what you may use. The 90 °C rating is chiefly for derating calculations.

Source Cerrowire chart temperature assignments; 90 °C caveat per the adopted code.

The basis these values assume

Not more than three current-carrying conductors in a raceway, cable or earth, at an ambient of 30 °C (86 °F). Bundle more conductors, or work in a hotter ambient, and the value must be adjusted — the adjustment and correction tables are in the code, not on this poster.

Aluminum is not a drop-in for copper. At 75 °C, 6 AWG is 65 A in copper but 50 A in aluminum; 4/0 is 230 A in copper but 180 A in aluminum.
