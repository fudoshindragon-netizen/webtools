Three investing calculators that only do arithmetic

Type your own numbers. The page multiplies and compounds them and shows you the result. It does not know what you should buy, it does not tell you what anything will return, and it does not name a single stock.

These are arithmetic tools, not advice. Nothing on this page is financial, investment, tax or legal advice, and nothing here is a recommendation, a suggestion or an opinion about any security, fund, strategy or course of action.

Every number you type is your own assumption. The results are what the arithmetic produces from those assumptions — they are not forecasts, not expected returns, and not predictions of what will happen. Real investments can lose money, and past results never repeat reliably.

No stock is named anywhere on this page — no tickers, no companies, no funds. The calculators work on share counts and prices you supply. Fees, taxes, inflation and price movement are not modelled. If you need advice about your own money, talk to a licensed professional who can see your full situation.

1. Compound growth

How this is calculated

Monthly rate i = rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100; months n = years × 12. Final balance = start × (1+i)n + monthly × ((1+i)n − 1) ÷ i. Total contributed = start + monthly × n. Growth = final balance − total contributed. If the rate is 0 the balance is simply start + monthly × n.

2. Dividend income

How this is calculated

Annual income = shares × dividend per share; monthly = annual ÷ 12. Position value = shares × price; yield = dividend per share ÷ price × 100. Later dividend per share = dividend × (1 + growth ÷ 100)years, and later annual income is that × shares. Dividends are not compounded into more shares, and tax and withholding are not modelled.

How this is calculated

Dollar risk = account × risk% ÷ 100. Risk per share = |entry − stop|. Shares = floor(dollar risk ÷ risk per share). Position dollars = shares × entry. Actual risk = shares × risk per share — always at or below the budget because of the rounding. A stop price is not a guarantee of the price you get filled at: gaps and slippage can lose more than the number shown.
